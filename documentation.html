Documentation Standard

The coordinator owns the quality and completeness of every note. This is what a finished note looks like, and how the day closes out with the two charting reports.

01

The Five Pillars of a Complete Note

A note is finished when all five are there. Four out of five is an incomplete note.

1Treatment Card Entry

What was done today. Wires, appliances, adjustments, what was placed or removed.

Reference →
2Treatment Notes

Treatment plan changes, updates, restorative needs, important dates, and any change to estimated treatment time.

Reference →
3Tooth Chart

The visual, annotated for anything that needs to be seen at a glance: restorative work, spaces we are holding open, primaries with no permanent replacement, elastics.

Reference →
4Next-Visit Note

Written in plain terms for whichever tech or doctor picks this patient up next. Where you can, write NV2 and NV3 too so the direction survives past one appointment.

5GPS Note

Four items, every time: how many weeks out to schedule, how many minutes to allocate, what type of appointment, and any special scheduling instruction the front desk needs about this patient.

02

What Makes a Note a Good Note

It gives a direction. Someone reading it should know what we are working on right now and what has to be true before we move on.

🧭
The one test that catches almost everything: "If I read this note cold next visit, would I know exactly what to do?" If the answer is no, it is not finished. More detail is better than less, and any dialogue with the patient gets documented.

Forbidden shorthand versus direction

Vague notes force the next tech to pull wires and wait for the doctor. Never leave these as a next-visit note:

ADJ check bite check "tooth" finishing bends consult for debond blank upgrade wires anything vague
Vague, forbiddenDirection, required
"Step down UR3 / check bite"Stepped down UR3 to close occlusion on the right canine. Next visit: evaluate vertical canine settling, check canine guidance, verify right buccal occlusion.
"Bends in lower wire / check bite"Stepped up LL4 and LL5 to level marginal ridges. Next visit: assess leveling of the lower arch and check buccal intercuspation.
"Consult for debond next visit"Begin pre-debond finishing. Verify before booking debond: 1.0 mm overbite and overjet, Class I canine relationship, complete space closure.
03

When Treatment Changes

A treatment plan change is never a conversation that lives only in the room.

◆
Anything that involves a change in treatment plan or new treatment gets written into the Treatment Notes, not just the card entry and not just the next-visit note, and it is reflected in the estimated treatment time so scheduling stays accurate.

Treatment time gets missed both ways. Some patients have a shortened timeline (prior treatment, a move, a target date) and the default drifts to six weeks and a slow wire sequence. Other patients land under four months from the end with no treatment plan in the notes at all. Keep the plan in the Treatment Notes, recall the patient at the interval the plan calls for, and review final objectives with pictures right after they get into finishing wires.

04

The Coordinator's Documentation Role

Your job with notes is review, not authorship. That distinction is the whole role.

You own quality and completeness

You audit every note for the five pillars and for real direction. You can help a tech work out what the next objectives are. What you do not do is write the note for them.

The tech authors, every time

The tech who saw the patient writes the note. If it comes back vague, it goes back to that tech to add direction before they leave. Doing it right the first time is the faster way home.

✓
AI scribes are tools, not authors. DentoAI and Keystone, our AI scribes, capture the conversation, but the clinical direction still has to land in the note. A draft missing direction is an unfinished note, and the tech owns it the moment it is signed.
05

End of Day: The Two Reports

Every clinic day closes with two charting reports. Both are signed off by the doctor before the team leaves, then saved.

Report 1 · Missing Entries

Run it to find any appointment without a completed entry. Resolve every gap before sign-off.

Report 2 · All Appointment Entries with Next-Visit Note

The full set of the day's entries with their next-visit notes, so the direction on every patient can be reviewed at a glance.

Doctor sign-off

Both reports are reviewed and signed off by the doctor at the end of the day.

Saved as PDF

Print both to PDF and save them in the shared folder for the end-of-day record.

Lab list current

Confirm the lab team has put every scan or lab case on the lab list, with the full appointment note attached.
