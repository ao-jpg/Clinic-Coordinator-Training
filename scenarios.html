Scenarios & Practice

Read each situation, decide what should happen, then reveal the expected behavior. Finish with the quiz at the bottom.

01

Coaching Scenarios

Ten everyday situations. No new clinical decisions, just the flow and documentation habits.

1 · Planned coverage
At the morning huddle, the team knows the coordinator will need to cover a chair for part of the morning.
Who is named as backup, and when does the handoff happen?
▶ Reveal
Expected: name the backup at huddle, verify they are actually available, and perform an explicit handoff before the coordinator begins treating.
2 · Sudden tech delay
A technician falls behind unexpectedly and the coordinator needs to step in.
What happens before the coordinator walks to the chair?
▶ Reveal
Expected: identify the next available person in the hierarchy, give the immediate status, receive acknowledgment, then leave the post.
3 · Child waiting, Chair 6 open
A child is waiting. Chair 6 is open, and a room is also available.
Should the child automatically go to Chair 6?
▶ Reveal
Expected: no. Look ahead. If the room is appropriate and not about to be needed for a higher-priority room patient, use the room.
4 · Chair 7 is empty
The clinic is busy and Chair 7 is empty.
Is Chair 7 simply the next chair to use?
▶ Reveal
Expected: no. Chair 7 is absolute overflow. Use normal chair and room strategy first.
5 · Routine adjustment arriving
The next-visit note shows a routine adjustment. The patient is not here yet.
What can be done before arrival?
▶ Reveal
Expected: select the chair, place the adjustment tray and safety glasses, open the chart, read the note, and prepare any clearly indicated items.
6 · Wire change
The next-visit note identifies a wire change.
What should the acting coordinator do before seating?
▶ Reveal
Expected: pull or place the planned wires from the note so the treating team does not have to set up after seating.
7 · Patient reports a poking wire
The patient is seated and says a wire is poking.
What should the acting coordinator do?
▶ Reveal
Expected: document the concern in the new visit note so the treating technician sees it.
8 · Hard leave-by time
The patient says, "I have to leave by 4:15."
What is the flow action?
▶ Reveal
Expected: communicate the timing need early to the coordinator and responsible team, and document it. A constraint only has value if the team learns it early enough to plan around it.
9 · Patient seated, no status change
The patient is in Chair 4 but the light bar is still neutral.
What is missing?
▶ Reveal
Expected: update Chair 4 to the yellow Chair Occupied status immediately.
10 · Patient comfortable
The patient is seated, status is yellow, the note is started, and concerns are documented.
What does the acting coordinator do next?
▶ Reveal
Expected: offer TV and comfort, excuse yourself, return to the open-bay computer, and identify who is next.
02

Quiz: Six Questions

Pick the best answer, then check. Each explains the common mistake and the fix.

Question 1
You need to cover a chair. What must happen before you leave the coordinator post?
Question 2
A child is waiting, Chair 6 is open, and a room is free and not needed soon. Where does the child go?
Question 3
A broken bracket with retractors in and a routine adjustment are both waiting for the doctor. Who goes first?
Question 4
A patient is seated in Chair 4 but the light bar has not changed. What is missing?
Question 5
A tech's note comes back vague, with no direction. As coordinator, what do you do?
Question 6
A tech radios in a broken bracket. What makes the call useful to you?
