Role Training

The Clinic Coordinator

The clinic coordinator owns patient flow and guards the quality of every chart that leaves the office. This is the training for both.

◆
The core rule: patient-flow ownership is never left unassigned. At any moment during clinic hours, the team should be able to answer one question instantly, with one clear name: "Who is Clinic Coordinator right now?" The name can change through the day. The responsibility never disappears between people.

Two jobs, in priority order

Everything on this site serves one of these two objectives.

Objective 1 · Most important

Patient Flow

Run the floor: watch the whole schedule, seat patients in the right chair at the right time, keep the light bar accurate, and clear traffic jams so the doctor keeps moving. When you must step away, hand the role off cleanly.

Quality & Documentation

What the role is

The clinic coordinator keeps awareness of the entire clinic rather than getting absorbed in one chair. As a rule, the coordinator is not routinely treating patients, because direct treatment removes the one person watching the whole system. The role is bigger than escorting a single patient: it is anticipating several patients at once, protecting room strategy, keeping the light bar current, and making sure the next arrival is already planned.

Schedule awareness

Always know who is here, who is next, and whether several patients will land close together.

Chair and room strategy

Choose the right location for each patient based on the appointment and near-term demand, not the first open chair.

Pre-arrival setup

Use the chart and next-visit note to prepare the chair before the patient sits.

Light-bar accuracy

Keep chair status current so the whole team can read flow at a glance.

Documentation quality

Review notes for completeness and direction. The coordinator owns quality, the tech owns authorship.

Clear the jams

Get the doctor unstuck, keep consults moving, and escalate constraints early.

!
The most common way flow collapses: the coordinator starts working directly with a patient and stops communicating with the team. When that happens, no one is directing flow and patients pile up. Communication never stops during patient hours. If you must go hands-on, hand off flow first.
