Preparing the Chair

Prepare everything that is reasonably predictable before the patient sits down. This removes dead time, keeps patients from watching us search for supplies, and lets the treating tech begin efficiently.

01

What "Plan Ahead" Means

Planning ahead means using information that already exists before the patient arrives. It does not mean guessing about treatment. Use the schedule and the documented next-visit note to make the chair ready for the expected procedure.

✓
Always begin the same way: open the chart in front of you and read the next-visit note. Everything you prepare should be supported by what that note says.
02

Pre-Arrival Preparation by Situation

Tap each to expand what "ready" looks like.

🪑Routine adjustment▶
Place an adjustment tray and a pair of patient safety sunglasses. Have the chart open and be ready with the next-visit plan.
🧵Wire change▶
If the note identifies new wires, pull or place the planned new wires so they are ready for the treating technician before seating.
💧Air / water / suction anticipated▶
Gather the appropriate air, water, and suction setup before seating so the tech is not assembling it after the patient is in the chair.
⚙️High-speed anticipated▶
Have the high-speed handpiece and the appropriate bur ready if the note indicates it will be needed.
➕Other predictable needs▶
Prepare anything the next-visit note clearly supports, rather than waiting until the patient is already seated.
03

Visual Standard: A Ready Adjustment Chair

Before the patient sits for a routine adjustment, this is the picture.

Pulled and placed per the note.

04

Quality Spot-Checks While Flow Runs

Preparing the chair is the front end of quality control. The back end is confirming every step of the system was completed, then adding an extra measure of quality before the patient leaves. This protects safety and the effectiveness of treatment, and it keeps small misses from becoming emergency visits.

Every step of the system was completed

Nothing in the sequence was skipped or left half done.

O-ties and steel ties in place

A missing o-tie or steel tie lets the wire disengage. It stalls the tooth movement we just set up and it is a common cause of avoidable emergency visits.

No poking wires, soft tissue correct

Catch a poking or long wire, and confirm soft tissue is where it should be, before the patient leaves the chair.

Documentation complete and directional

Complete, up to standard, and written with real direction. (See Documentation.)

!
Spot-checking is review, not takeover. You are making sure the work and the note meet standard, not doing the tech's job for them.
